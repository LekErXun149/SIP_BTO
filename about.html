Why this exists

Buying a first flat is one of the largest financial decisions a young adult in Singapore will make — and the information needed to make it well is scattered, dense, and written for people who already understand it.

Most people meet the BTO process one confusing piece at a time. What the income ceiling is this year. Whether a grant applies. What Plus actually commits you to. What an application rate of 3.2 means for your chances. By the time the pieces fit together, decisions have often already been made.

KeyQuest puts those pieces in one place and makes them personal rather than abstract.

The numbers, for you

Not what a Mortgage Servicing Ratio is, but whether your figures pass it — including the gap between the rate you pay and the higher rate HDB tests you against.

The odds, honestly

Take the live application rate from HDB's own portal and see what it means, including why the same flats look completely different to a first-timer and a second-timer.

A way forward either way

If you're over the income ceiling, or the odds look impossible, there's a page of real alternatives rather than a dead end.

Where the figures come from

Everything is sourced

Housing policy in Singapore moves quickly. During the few months this site was built, the income ceiling rose, HDB restructured their entire website, and a long-standing priority scheme was replaced.

Because of that, every figure on this site links back to the official HDB page it came from, and the footer carries the date they were last checked. Where something is an assumption rather than a published figure, it says so where it appears.

The ballot tool goes a step further and stores no application rates at all — you read the live figure from HDB's portal and enter it, so it can't quietly go stale.

What this can't tell you

KeyQuest is an educational tool. It is not financial, legal or housing advice, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by HDB.

The calculators work from the figures you enter and the rules published at the time of writing. They can't account for your full financial position, your CPF balances, or the details HDB assesses when processing a real application. The ballot tool explains what an application rate means; it cannot predict an outcome, because the ballot is a random draw.

Your HFE letter is the figure that counts. It's HDB's own assessment of what you can buy, what grants you qualify for, and how much you can borrow. Verify anything here against hdb.gov.sg before making decisions.

Academic context

A Social Innovation Project

KeyQuest was built by students at the Singapore Institute of Technology for USI2001, a module on addressing real societal challenges through inter-disciplinary collaboration.

How might we help first-time BTO applicants aged 21–35 understand downpayment, grants and affordability trade-offs so that they can plan confidently and realistically?

Young Singaporeans are increasingly eager to plan for home ownership, in a country with one of the world's highest home ownership rates. But complex terminology, varying grant eligibility criteria, uncertainty around downpayments, and the introduction of the Standard, Plus and Prime framework all make buying a first flat a daunting prospect. This project explores how accessible, engaging financial guidance can help young applicants make informed, realistic and confident decisions.
